# PRD-140 — The diffusion solver's step count: a Krylov exponential instead of four thousand sweeps

**Status**: open, measured (2026-09-08). Raised while optimising the GPU
stencil, when it became clear that the remaining wins were factors and the
step count was orders of magnitude. Not scheduled.

## The question

`IMP::bff::diffusion_propagate` takes four thousand explicit Euler sweeps to
cover 50 ns of donor decay, because forward Euler on a diffusion operator is
stable only for `dt <= dg^2/(6D)`. That bound is why the solver costs `dg^-5`,
why halving the voxel edge is 32× the work, why calibration wants a surrogate
(`quench_pinn`, PRD-135), and why every gradient in the quenching stack is a
finite difference through a multi-second solve.

Optimising the sweep has been worth 13-14× on the GPU
(`imp.bff/okf/validation/gpu_diffusion_is_worth_it.md`). Should the *number*
of sweeps be attacked instead?

## What was measured

`imp.bff/benchmark/large_time_steps.py`, ng = 41, against
`scipy.sparse.linalg.expm_multiply` — the exact solution of the semi-discrete
equation. Detail in `imp.bff/okf/validation/larger_time_steps.md`.

**The budget is 2.04e-6**: that is the shipped scheme's own error, first-order
Lie splitting plus forward Euler. And there is no slack in `dt` — `rho(A+K)`
is 1.530 against a stability limit of 2, so the shipped step already sits at
77% of the bound.

| method | operator applications | vs 4 000 | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped (forward Euler + Lie splitting) | 4 000 | 1× | 2.04e-6 |
| RKL2 super-time-stepping, s = 6 | 1 938 | **2.1×** | 8.1e-7 |
| Krylov (Lanczos) exponential, m = 64 | 64 | **62×** | 3.7e-7 (f32) |
| Krylov (Lanczos) exponential, m = 128 | 128 | **31×** | 3.5e-7 (f32) |

RKL2 is limited by accuracy rather than stability: every stage count is
stable, but past s = 6 the second-order error exceeds what ships today.

The Krylov figure holds across both flux forms, a point-source as well as a
smooth start, and a quencher raising `k` by 300× in a four-voxel ball; the
hard cases need m = 128 rather than 64. It needs no reorthogonalisation
(measured identical to fourteen digits), runs in f32, and for the
fluorescence trace needs no stored basis at all — three vectors and one scalar
per Krylov step.

## Why this operator allows it

* **Constant in time.** `d`, `decay` and `bounds` do not change over a
  propagation, so the whole run is one matrix exponential rather than a
  sequence of different problems.
* **Symmetrisable.** Smoluchowski is symmetric as it stands; Ito is not, but
  `D^(1/2) A D^(-1/2)` is symmetric to 6e-17, so `q = sqrt(d) p` brings it into
  the same machinery.
* **`m` scales as the square root of the stiffness.** Doubling the step count
  costs ~1.4× more Krylov vectors where the explicit scheme costs 2×, so the
  advantage grows exactly where the solver hurts.

## Decisions this needs from the owner

1. **Is a more accurate answer acceptable?** The Krylov route does not
   reproduce today's numbers; it is better than them by about 2e-6. Pinned
   test values move. This is a behaviour change even though it is an
   improvement.
2. **Second path or replacement?** A second numerical path needs a permanent
   parity gate, which is the same problem the GPU backend already has.
3. **Does the interface change?** The method produces the decay as a sum of
   `m` exponentials with explicit rates and amplitudes — which is the form a
   fluorescence decay is fitted with. "A trace sampled every 100 steps" may
   not be the right output any more.
4. **The adjoint.** `diffusion_propagate_adjoint` is checkpointed reverse-mode
   over the sweeps. With a symmetric operator the adjoint is the same
   operator, so the same machinery serves — but it has not been worked out,
   and the parameter gradients `dL/dd`, `dL/ddecay` are currently local
   products of forward and adjoint states at each step, which a method with no
   steps does not have.

## The one open risk

On the GPU the shape of the computation changes. Lanczos needs one fused
reduction per matvec, and at ng = 81 a reduction measures ~90 us against ~21 us
for the stencil — so 128 Krylov steps would be dominated by reductions, not by
the kernel that was just tuned. Estimated ~14 ms against 98 ms today, i.e. ~7×
on the GPU and ~100× against the threaded CPU. **That estimate is the first
thing to measure**, because it decides whether the GPU work and this compose
or compete.

## What was rejected, and why

- **Leapfrog and other centred-in-time schemes**: unstable for parabolic
  equations at every step size. DuFort-Frankel repairs the stability but is
  only conditionally consistent.
- **Crank-Nicolson with CG**: unpreconditioned CG needs about `sqrt(kappa)`
  iterations per step, the same order as RKL2's stage count, so it competes
  with 2.1× rather than with 31×.
- **ADI**: tridiagonal sweeps along lines suit a GPU badly and an irregular
  domain worse.
