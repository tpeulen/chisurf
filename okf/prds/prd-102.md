# Gigastructures in ChiMOL

## The objective, stated

tpeulen, 2026-08-12: **YASARA's performance is the baseline ChiMOL has to
match.** A gigastructure is the test case, and ChiMOL must handle systems up to
**30x the size of the NPC** — the NPC demo being the thing that is still slow
today. Two further asks came with it: **support pet molecules**, and be able to
**assemble gigastructures in ChiMOL using IMP**.

## The objective, restated 2026-08-12: a ladder from atoms to micrometres

tpeulen, correcting the framing: **Martini is not the pet layer, but it is *a*
layer.** The objective is a **continuous ladder of representations spanning
about five orders of magnitude** — roughly 1 angstrom to 10 micrometres:

| rung | representation | reduction | good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **atoms** | 1:1 | chemistry, contacts, the thing itself |
| 2 | **Martini beads** | ~9-11 heavy atoms per bead | physics that is still real |
| 3 | **super-beads / Gaussians** | tens to hundreds of atoms | shape at assembly scale |
| 4 | **splats / continuum** | whole objects | micrometre scenes |

And the reason to believe it is possible, in tpeulen's words: *"I know something
like this was achieved with Gaussian splats on another length scale (km to mm),
so it must be possible. Research also in other directions, outside of
molecules, on other length scales."*

That instruction is the useful part of this PRD's scope. The dynamic-range
problem is **not** specific to molecules — planetary renderers, point-cloud
renderers and virtualised-geometry systems all solve "span many orders of
magnitude without the near field breaking or the far field costing anything",
and they have solved it for longer. **Two traps are known to live there and are
worth stating before any design**: floating-point precision (spanning five
orders of magnitude breaks float32 position and depth, which is why planetary
renderers rebase the origin camera-relative), and the transition criterion
between rungs, which is where popping and double-counting live.

This supersedes the earlier reading in which Martini *was* the coarse
representation. It is rung 2 of four, and the compression the mesoscale needs
lives in rungs 3 and 4.

## What the baseline actually is

From YASARA's own pages, so the target is a number rather than an impression:

* A model of the **presynaptic bouton with 3.6 billion atoms**, visualised
  **interactively** on a single **GeForce RTX 2080**.
* More than **4 GB of graphics memory** for the largest structures.
* The reduction comes from two independent compressions, and the factors are
  quoted separately: **coarse-graining ~50x**, **GPU instancing 40-1000x**.

That last line is the whole design. Neither trick alone reaches 3.6 billion:
50x off 3.6e9 is still 72 million, which no immediate-mode renderer draws
interactively. It is the *product* — coarse-graining the geometry and then
drawing each distinct piece once per frame however many copies exist — that
closes the gap. Any plan that adopts one and defers the other will miss the
target by roughly the factor it deferred.

## What a pet molecule is

YASARA's **"pet world"**: molecules scaled to **1/10th** their normal size and
represented by **pet atoms** — coarse-grained beads that need only **~2% of the
original atom count**. Beads are coloured by size, 0.2 to 1.2 Angstrom, so the
simplification is legible rather than hidden.

Two properties matter for scoping, and both are easy to get wrong:

1. **It is a modelling representation, not an approximation of dynamics.**
   YASARA is explicit that pet-world simulation is *not* a faster replacement
   for all-atom simulation — it is **aimed at model building only**. A pet
   structure is how you *assemble* and *inspect* something enormous; it is not
   a cheaper way to compute its physics. ChiMOL should carry the same warning
   at the point of use, or someone will publish a pet-world energy.
2. **The scale factor is part of the representation.** Coordinates live at 1/10
   size. ChiMOL already has a scene scale factor of 10 and a documented trap
   around it (a radius of gyration read off scene coordinates came out 10x
   too large). Pet coordinates entering that pipeline are a second factor of
   ten waiting to multiply with the first.

## Measured on this machine, 2026-08-12

The feasibility question is settled, and it was settled by measurement on the
actual target (Apple M1 Pro, 14-core GPU, 16 GiB unified, Metal via wgpu-py
0.32.0) rather than by argument. Ray-cast sphere impostors, 1920x1080, depth
test on:

| particles | flat | two-level instanced |
|---|---|---|
| 234,184 (the NPC) | 0.94 ms | 1.50 ms |
| **7,009,000 (30x the NPC)** | — | **22.9 ms (44 fps)** |
| 49,880,000 | — | 161 ms |
| 99,760,000 | — | 406 ms |

**The 30x target is already within reach of this GPU, before any LOD or
culling.** Two-level instancing is ~2x faster per particle *and* ~240x smaller
(1 MB against 252 MB at 7 M), because a small shared template stays in cache.

### The three findings that change the plan

**1. The bottleneck is the CPU, not the GPU — by three orders of magnitude.**
chimol's own record has the NPC at 1030 ms/frame *of which the molecule drew in
2 ms*, and this session measured the same shape from the other end: the frame is
**94 % chrome**, with `_chrome_quads` at 37.8 ms of a 41 ms frame while 234,184
beads cost about 1 ms. So the first-order win for "the NPC is slow" is
**per-repaint CPU work**, not the renderer. Building a GPU-driven pipeline first
would optimise the 2 ms and leave the 1030.

**2. Instancing does not buy frame rate — it buys memory.** It reduces build
time, residency and cache pressure by the copy count, but **7 M beads is 7 M
rasterised quads either way**. YASARA's own page says the same thing: its
40-1000x for instancing is quoted as a *memory* factor. The frame-rate factor
comes from coarse-graining, i.e. LOD. The number to design against is Mol*'s
published ablation: **without LOD its billion-atom scene slows by 10-26x**.
The earlier framing in this PRD — that instancing is "the larger factor" —
was wrong, and is corrected here.

**3. The headline atom counts in this literature are *effective*, not
*resident*.** cellVIEW's 15 billion is a 60 M-atom model repeated 250x: 60 M
resident, 40 unique templates, 100,000 instances, a few hundred MB. Its
predecessor is sharper still — 30 billion effective, **13,092,630 atoms actually
emitted**, a ~2,300x reduction. Mesoscale rendering is won by **not emitting
geometry**. Any plan measured against a resident billion atoms is measuring the
wrong thing.

### WebGPU on Metal: what is and is not available

Verified on this adapter: instanced draw, `draw_indirect`, `indirect-first-instance`,
render bundles, ray-query, subgroups, timestamp queries. A compute frustum-cull
with atomic compaction over 8 M particles took **2.81 ms** and fed
`draw_indirect` directly, bit-exact against NumPy — so GPU-driven culling is
reachable today.

Not available, and both change the shape of a design:

* **No multi-draw-indirect** on Metal through wgpu. Loop `draw_indirect`, or
  use render bundles.
* **No conservative depth.** WGSL has no equivalent of `sv_depthgreaterequal`,
  which cellVIEW names as its key optimisation, so an impostor writing
  `@builtin(frag_depth)` loses early-Z. Measured cost on this M1: **1.2x** for
  sub-pixel spheres, **1.9x** for close-up overdraw. Not fatal; budget for it.

Two limits worth knowing: `max_storage_buffer_binding_size` is 4 GiB (split a
larger arena across bindings), and `max_compute_workgroups_per_dimension` is
65,535 — so a workgroup-256 1-D dispatch **tops out at 16,776,960 items** and a
cull pass beyond ~16.7 M particles needs a 2-D dispatch. That is an easy cliff
to miss.

Unified memory removes YASARA's ">4 GB of graphics memory" gate rather than
satisfying it: there is no fixed VRAM wall, but GPU allocations compete with the
OS for the same 16 GB, so the practical budget is a few GB. Since the resident
figures across this whole literature are tens of MB to a few hundred MB, that is
not the constraint.

### The ladder has been built before, for molecules

**Read this before designing rung 3.** Parulek, Jonsson, Ropinski, Bruckner,
Ynnerman & Viola, *"Continuous Levels-of-Detail and Visual Abstraction for
Seamless Molecular Visualization"*, CGF 33(6):276-287,
[10.1111/cgf.12349](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.12349).

It is rungs 1-3 of the table above, in one 2014 paper, and it already uses the
word **superatom**. The level is a **continuous field over space, not a discrete
switch**: near/mid/far fields with a transition band driven by camera distance,
blending **SES surface -> Gaussian/blobby surface -> sphere billboards**, plus
hierarchical clustering into superatoms. Measured 5-10x, and 20x with the
hierarchy; cost is **linear in clusters rendered**.

The reason it matters more than its age suggests: **ChiMOL already has all three
of its representations** — marching-cubes SES, Wyvill metaballs, and sphere
impostors. What is missing is the continuous blend between them, not the
representations. A continuous field is also a better fit than discrete rungs,
because it makes the transition criterion a parameter rather than a decision.

Its own reported limits are worth carrying: clustering is **expensive
precompute** (up to 80 s for a 58,674-atom complex), and the cluster-hierarchy
traversal ran **on the CPU every frame** — which is precisely the cost cellVIEW
later moved into a compute shader, and precisely the class of cost this PRD's
own measurements say dominates ChiMOL today.

And from the other end, cellVIEW's authors state in their own paper that the
particle representation has a ceiling, exactly where rung 4 begins: *"when
viewing our largest dataset in its entirety, the view starts to exhibit graining
artefacts due to the very small screen-size of individual molecules ...
therefore another type of approach rather than the particle-based one should be
considered in this case."* That is primary-source confirmation, from the team
that reached 15 billion atoms at 60 fps, that rung 4 cannot be spheres.

### Three constraints measured or verified on this M1

**1. There are no 64-bit atomics — and this gates the GPU-driven designs.**
Verified on this adapter: `shader-int64` is present, but
**`shader-int64-atomic-min-max` and `shader-int64-atomic-all-ops` are not**;
WGSL atomics are 32-bit. Both of the mechanisms most worth borrowing depend on a
64-bit atomic over a packed depth:payload integer — Nanite's visibility buffer
(**30 bits depth : 27 bits cluster : 7 bits triangle**, where the max-atomic
*is* the depth test) and Schutz's point renderer (depth:index `atomicMin`).
Neither transplants as written. The fallbacks are a 32-bit packing (24-bit depth
: 8-bit payload), a two-pass depth-then-ID scheme, or simply the ordinary
pipeline with hardware depth. **Settle this before committing to any rung-3/4
architecture.**

**2. Depth precision is the first thing to fix, and it is nearly free.**
The span in question is a **10^5 near/far ratio**, which is exactly the ratio at
which an independent benchmark scores reversed-Z with a float depth buffer at
**0 % indistinguishable and 0 % swapped**, against 45 % / 18 % for the standard
setup. The nuance to carry: **float32 and int24 depth are near-equivalent under
standard-Z** — a float depth buffer pays *only* in combination with reversed-Z.
So it is one change, not two: **reversed-Z plus `depth32float` plus an infinite
far plane**. `depth32float-stencil8` is available on this adapter.

**3. Positions probably need no machinery at all.** float32 carries 10 um with
about two orders of headroom. Where more is wanted, the cheap answer is not
RTC/RTE but **fixed-point relative to a local origin**: Potree stores XYZ as
3 x uint32 relative to the node's bounding-box minimum — **4,294 km at
millimetre precision**, four and a half orders more than this objective needs.
The adaptive version quantises to 30 bits relative to each *batch's* bbox in
three 10-bit planes, loading the high plane only, for **4 bytes per point**. Its
failure mode is visible rather than subtle: 10 bits over a 3.7 km batch gives
3.6 m steps and *"visible stripe patterns"*; 20 bits is indistinguishable from
30.

**4. For rung 3/4 topology, prefer a VDB to an octree.** Measured in Museth's
own tables: a `[7,6,5,4,3]` configuration indexes **33,554,432^3 — 7.5 orders of
magnitude — in 439 MB**, against 422 MB for `[6,5,4,3]` at 262,144^3. Seven extra
octaves for a **4 % memory penalty and identical cached access time**. An octree
lookup costs 50-70x the cached VDB lookup, and this objective's 10^5 span is
16.6 octaves, i.e. **17 octree levels** — one deeper than the deepest published
sparse-voxel-octree scene, and 17 dependent memory hops per random access. Two
caveats: a VDB is explicitly **not a mip pyramid** (its tiles are constant
regions, not filtered downsamples), so a cone-footprint ladder must be built on
top; and the level count should follow a **measured local dimensionality** —
molecular *surfaces* give an affordable ladder, space-filling density does not.
NanoVDB is Apache-2.0.

**On anti-popping**, there is an analytic option that costs nothing and is exact:
`t_morph = clamp(2*rho/tau - 1, 0, 1)` is 0 exactly where the parent splits and
1 exactly where the chunk splits, so the geometry at a transition is *identical*
on both sides — eliminated rather than hidden. Prefer it on the sphere rungs and
reserve alpha-interpolation for an anisotropic rung.

### Prior art, and what may actually be borrowed

**Mol\* is the only prior art here that can be borrowed as *code*.** It is
**MIT**, actively maintained, and the closest architectural match — and its
Mesoscale Explorer renders a **3.07-billion-atom presynaptic bouton in >577,000
instances**, at ~14-23 ms on a desktop and **~70 ms on an integrated Intel Iris
Plus**. An integrated GPU doing billions of atoms in a browser is the most
encouraging data point available for an M1 target. Note it has **no WebGPU
backend** yet (planned) — all its numbers are WebGL.

Its LOD trick is worth copying outright and is almost free: the sphere buffer is
**reordered coarsest-first by stride**, storing cumulative offsets, so selecting
a LOD level is **literally truncating `drawCount`** — one buffer, no re-upload,
about half the memory. Remaining spheres are grown to preserve space-filling
volume, and fading is either radius scaling or a dithered Bayer `discard` that
TAA accumulates into stochastic transparency — order-independent, no sorting.
Symmetry is discovered by **content-addressed equivalence** rather than declared,
so a 60x capsid collapses to one geometry and `instanceCount = 60` with nothing
telling the renderer it is an assembly.

Licences, checked: **ChimeraX is off-limits for code** — a UCSF *non-commercial*
licence that forbids redistribution, with 1,036 files additionally under
LGPL-2.1; more restrictive than GPL for our purposes, so read it for
architecture only. **cellVIEW** is unlicensed (`matmuze/cellVIEW`) or GPL-3.0
(`cellVIEW-i`); the URL printed in its paper is dead. **cellPACK**'s MIT claim is
contaminated by GPL-3.0-or-later headers inherited from autoPACK.

For the record on what ChimeraX actually does, since it was named as the
reference to read: no impostors at all (real triangulated spheres, instanced at
16 bytes each), no frustum or occlusion culling, LOD by *shrinking the mesh*
(2000 down to a floor of 10 triangles per atom against a 5 M-triangle budget),
and above 1 M atoms it simply **stops drawing atoms**. It has no
coarse-graining machinery, and no maximum atom count is documented anywhere in
its source or docs.

## The parts

Three were planned; a fourth -- the splat rung as an actual pipeline -- was
folded in on 2026-08-13 and is below.

### 1. Instancing over the copy hierarchy

The NPC is many copies of a few distinct components; a gigastructure is that
taken much further. The change is to stop treating a copy as unique geometry:
**one built mesh per distinct type, drawn N times with per-instance
transforms**, rather than N built meshes. That collapses build time *and* VRAM
by the copy count, which is the only kind of factor that reaches 30x.

Prerequisite: the scene must carry a *hierarchy* with instance transforms
rather than a flat list of objects. Read **ChimeraX** (`junk/ChimeraX`) for how
a molecular viewer does this at scale before designing — it is already this
plugin's reference for density-map contouring, and its `CHISURF-*` headers
record what has been taken from it.

### 2. The pet representation

A bead per group of atoms at 1/10 scale, drawn with the existing analytic
sphere impostor (spheres are already 1,299 quads and 0 triangles for 148L, so
the primitive is right). What is new is the *reduction*: which atoms map to
which bead, and the radius/colour rule.

### 3. Assembly, in imp-tricks — not here

Building a gigastructure rather than only importing one. **tpeulen, 2026-08-12:
this is to be implemented in imp-tricks**, and that is a scope boundary rather
than a preference. It follows the rule the project already applies to every
other piece of molecular modelling — AV, labels, restraints, Structure,
ProteinMC, kinetics all left chisurf for `IMP.cgmol.*` / `IMP.bff.*` — and it
draws the seam in the useful place:

* **imp-tricks builds.** Coarse-grained representation, packing, collision
  detection, restraints, sampling. It already owns IMP.
* **ChiMOL displays and drives.** It loads what imp-tricks writes and gives the
  operation a viewport, a command and a progress report. It does not learn to
  pack.

The practical consequence is that the *interchange format* is the real interface
between the two, and it needs deciding early: whatever imp-tricks emits has to
carry the instance hierarchy (part 1) rather than a flattened particle list, or
the assembler will destroy exactly the structure the renderer needs.

**Leverage Martini** (tpeulen, 2026-08-12), and imp-tricks already has Martini
support to start from. The assessment was done — findings below, and two of them
change the plan.

#### Martini is not a pet-molecule substitute

This is the load-bearing result, and it contradicts the obvious reading of the
brief. **There is no coarser-than-4:1 Martini.** Neither Dry Martini (implicit
solvent) nor GōMartini 3 changes the mapping, and the S and T bead classes go the
*finer* direction — 3:1 and 2:1 — not coarser. Martini 3's effective compression
measured against real published systems is **9–11 heavy atoms per bead**
(triply sourced: a whole cell at 10.7, SARS-CoV-2 at 8.9, and 561 M beads against
>6 G atoms at ~11, water dominating because one `W` bead is four waters).

YASARA's pet world claims ~2 % of the atom count, i.e. roughly **50:1**. Martini
is therefore about **5x weaker compression**, and no configuration of it closes
that gap. A plan that adopts Martini *as* the pet representation misses the
target by that factor.

The Marrink lab's own route above Martini resolution is to **leave particle-based
modelling entirely**: a dynamically triangulated continuum surface
([FreeDTS](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-44819-w)), backmapped down into
Martini beads by [TS2CG](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-16094-y). The
ecosystem's answer above Martini is a **mesh**, not a smaller bead.

So the design is **two levels**, which is what the instancing section was already
reaching toward:

* **display / assembly level** — meshes and instance transforms, ChiMOL's pet
  analogue, where the 50x lives;
* **Martini level** — beads with real parameters, for anything physical.

If a genuinely coarser *force field* is wanted rather than a display
representation, the documented precedent is
[SMARTINI3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-75490-2) (~3 beads per lipid
against Martini's ~12 for POPC), explicitly built so Martini proteins embed in an
ultra-CG bilayer. Note it is Risselada's group, not Marrink's.

#### The assembly tool already exists: bentopy

This is what "there was already a tool" refers to. **bentopy** — Westendorp,
Stevens, Brown, Dommer, Wassenaar, Bruininks & Marrink, *Protein Science* 35(3):e70480
(2026), [10.1002/pro.70480](https://doi.org/10.1002/pro.70480) — packs large
cellular scenes and emits a **`placements.json`**: a list of *which component goes
where*. That is an instance list, which is precisely the input part 1 needs, so
adopting it and consuming `placements.json` gets the assembler and the instance
hierarchy in one move. Do not write a packer.

Division of labour: **bentopy places**, **imp-tricks builds the components and
refines with IMP restraints**, **ChiMOL displays the instances**.

#### The physics claim, stated precisely

A Martini-shaped model is a candidate for simulation **only via an exported
GROMACS topology**, and *not* via imp-tricks' in-tree scoring as currently
written. The assessment found the in-tree interaction table is a five-value
lookup on a bead's first letter, where real Martini 3 has thousands of distinct
epsilon values — every one of P1…P6 collapses to one number. That is not an
approximation of Martini 3, it is a different force field. State the seam; do not
let a picture imply an energy.

#### Licensing

Martini 3 parameters are **Apache 2.0** via
[marrink-lab/martini-forcefields](https://github.com/marrink-lab/martini-forcefields).
**cgmartini.nl carries no license at all** — vendor and cite from the GitHub
grant, never the website. Pin the *molecule* parameter set, not "Martini 3": the
bead interaction matrix has not moved since v3.0.0 in 2021 and there are no
tagged releases, while the lipid and IDP parameter sets move independently.

YASARA's own assembly path is the shape to aim at: nucleic acids and whole
genomes built **directly from a FASTA file**, optionally with secondary structure
in **dot-bracket** notation, with efficient collision detection producing compact
packings.

## 4. The splat rung, as a pipeline (folded in 2026-08-13)

A design brief for rung 4, supplied as planning material and recorded here
because it is the first concrete account of *how* the continuum rung is built
rather than what it is for. Nothing below is implemented; it is the shape to
argue with.

The claim it rests on is the one already in this PRD: a hierarchical,
band-limited, page-streamed representation is how other fields span five orders
of magnitude, and 3D Gaussian splatting is the version of it with a mature
open-source record. The named references are `graphdeco-inria/gaussian-splatting`
(the parameterisation), `Mip-Splatting` and `gsplat` (scale-space filtering),
`m-schuetz/GaussianSplats3D` and `playcanvas/super-splat` (octree chunking and
page packing), `antimatter15/splat` and the SOG work (quantisation), and
`kwokcb/webgpu-gaussian-splatting` (WGSL storage layout). **Read for algorithm
and architecture; licences are to be checked before anything is taken.**

**Added 2026-08-13:** `scttfrdmn/webgpu-compute-exploration` (MIT) is a
different kind of reference — not a splatting or paging system, a small
teaching showcase of ten independent WebGPU compute examples in plain WGSL,
including a Lennard-Jones molecular-dynamics example (Velocity Verlet,
instanced spheres, up to 5,000 particles at 60 fps, O(N^2) force calc entirely
on the GPU) and a Game-of-Life example that is the simplest available worked
case of `atomicAdd`-style compute-to-render coordination. Useful for a minimal,
readable WGSL pattern to check a compute or instancing approach against before
writing it against the harder Nanite/cellVIEW/Mol* references above — not
useful for scale, since its own numbers (thousands of particles, a 64^3 grid)
are three to four orders of magnitude below this PRD's target and it does not
address paging, quantisation or LOD at all.

### 4.1 Scale-space band-limiting and parent folding

Per octree node *N_k* at depth *d*, over child Gaussians `{g_i}`:

1. take the base parameters `(mu, Sigma, alpha, SH)`;
2. combine children into a centroid `mu_parent` and covariance `Sigma_parent`;
3. **low-pass in 3D** so zooming out does not alias:
   `Sigma_parent' = Sigma_parent + sigma_filter^2(d) * I`;
4. derive per-primitive transition thresholds `(S_min, S_max)` on the
   *screen-space footprint*, and blend continuously rather than switching:
   `alpha_rendered = alpha * clamp((S_pixel - S_min) / (S_max - S_min), 0, 1)`.

Step 3 is the one that matters and the one a naive implementation omits: a
parent that is merely the average of its children **aliases** on zoom-out, which
is exactly the popping this PRD already names as where the transition criterion
goes wrong. Step 4 is what makes the LOD *continuous* — the alpha ramp is the
mechanism, and `(S_min, S_max)` is where double-counting between rungs will show
up if the ranges of parent and child overlap wrongly.

### 4.2 Morton order and virtual pages

Sort folded Gaussians along a Morton (or Hilbert) curve on their centroids, then
cut the sorted run into fixed-size **virtual pages** — 4,096 primitives is the
reference figure. The page index table carries, per page: `Page_ID` (u32), the
bounding box (`float3[2]`), `LOD_Level` (u8), and `Parent_Page_ID` /
`Child_Page_IDs` (u32).

The point of the space-filling curve is that a page is then also a *spatial*
unit, so a frustum test on the page bound is meaningful and a page fetch brings
in primitives that will be needed together.

### 4.3 Quantisation, 12-16 bytes per primitive

- **position** — u16[3], relative to the page bounding box (which is why the
  box is in the page table and not merely descriptive);
- **rotation** — 32-bit smallest-three quaternion (2-bit index + 3x10-bit);
- **scale** — u8[3] on log-scales;
- **opacity** — u8, sigmoid-mapped;
- **SH** — DC term as 8-bit RGB; higher orders through a per-page vector
  quantisation codebook.

Note the interaction with the ladder's known precision trap: quantising
position *relative to the page box* is the same move as a planetary renderer's
camera-relative rebasing, one level down. It is what buys five orders of
magnitude out of 16-bit integers.

### 4.4 WebGPU storage and traversal

A WGSL `struct` matching the packed layout for `var<storage, read>`, with
shader entry points for frustum culling, per-primitive `S_pixel` evaluation, and
an **atomic page-request queue** (`atomicAdd`) that the host drains to decide
what to stream. Host side: LRU slot allocation over a fixed VRAM budget, and a
radix sort dispatch for back-to-front blending.

### 4.5 Deliverables, if this is built

1. a JSON manifest — page table, tree pointers, `(S_min, S_max)`, byte offsets;
2. the binary struct in both C++ and WGSL, byte-for-byte agreeing;
3. a written traversal contract — page fetch, LRU eviction, sort dispatch.

### What has to be settled before any of it

- **Does this rung serve molecules or replace them?** Splats are a *display*
  representation with no chemistry: a splat cannot be selected, measured or
  labelled. The ladder's rungs 1-3 stay addressable; rung 4 is where a scene
  stops being a model. That boundary needs stating in the UI, or someone will
  measure a distance between two blobs.
- **Where do the Gaussians come from?** A trained 3DGS scene is fitted to
  photographs; here the input is a structure. Folding atoms or beads into
  Gaussians analytically (a Gaussian *is* an atom's density) is a different
  operation from training one, and probably cheaper and better conditioned --
  but it is unproven in this stack.
- **Does it compose with instancing?** Part 1 collapses cost by drawing one
  mesh N times. A page-streamed splat tree is a *different* traversal, and the
  two must not each assume they own the draw loop.
- **The measurement, before anything.** This PRD already insists the NPC's
  load time, frame time and VRAM are recorded first. That applies here twice
  over: 12-16 bytes per primitive is a claim about VRAM, and VRAM is the
  budget the whole page-streaming design exists to respect.

## 5. Draft idea: attention-coupled dynamic simulation (2026-08-13, not scoped)

Everything above is a *viewer* problem — an existing, static structure drawn
at a rung chosen by screen-space footprint. The idea recorded here is
different in kind and unproven: **the scene need not be static.** If ChiMOL is
running live dynamics (or a live assembly) across the ladder rather than
displaying a frozen structure, the same footprint signal that already selects
a *display* rung could select a *simulation* rung — full physics only where
something is actually being looked at, coarser or frozen elsewhere, coupling
compute cost to attention the way this PRD already couples draw cost to it.

The precedent is in-tree and already shipped, one repo over: `tttrlib`'s
`SimEngine` (`modules/simulation/include/SimEngine.h`) runs its CLSM photon
simulation exactly this way. Per molecule, `focus_gap()` measures distance to
the effective-focus AABB (the region where excitation intensity is above a
threshold fraction of peak); inside the focus a molecule is fine-stepped so
its photon statistics are correct, and far from it the same molecule
*coasts* — advanced with a step sized by its distance to the nearest boundary,
because outside the focus `Iex = 0` and no photon can be produced, so the
expensive per-step field lookup is provably wasted work. `run_independent()`
goes further: each molecule's whole timeline is simulated independently
end-to-end, coasting-then-fine-stepping on its own schedule, embarrassingly
parallel across molecules. The shape to borrow is the *coupling*, not the
optics: a cheap, exact test for "does this region affect what is currently
being observed", and two step costs gated on its answer, rather than one
uniform step everywhere.

What would have to be answered before this is more than an idea:

- **What is "attention" here?** A confocal simulation has one unambiguous
  answer, the beam focus. A viewport has several candidates — camera frustum,
  a fixation point, the current selection, an explicit region a user pins —
  and they are not interchangeable: a frustum test is cheap and geometric, a
  fixation point is a UX decision, and either can disagree with what part 4's
  `S_pixel` LOD is already choosing for *drawing*. Whether one signal drives
  both draw-rung and simulate-rung, or they are deliberately independent,
  needs deciding before either is built.
- **What does "coarser" mean for dynamics, not for display?** Rung 4's splats
  have no chemistry to simulate at all; rung 3's superatoms and rung 2's
  Martini beads presumably do, at their own resolution. Coasting a molecule in
  `SimEngine` skips *photon* work because the physics genuinely does not
  change outside the focus (`Iex = 0` is exact, not approximate). The
  out-of-attention analogue for structural dynamics — freeze, extrapolate, or
  step down a rung — is not the same kind of exact and has not been picked.
- **Does it compose with the splat pipeline's own paging?** Part 4 already
  streams geometry in and out of VRAM by camera distance. A second system
  streaming *simulation* fidelity in and out by the same or a different
  signal is two independent LOD controllers over the same scene; whether they
  share one signal or genuinely need two is unresolved.
- **No measurement yet.** Unlike every other section in this PRD, nothing here
  has been benchmarked, prototyped, or even scoped to a test case. This is a
  parking spot for the idea, not a design.

## 6. Draft idea: a coordinate pyramid for 500 um-to-angstrom without losing f32 accuracy (2026-08-13, not scoped)

tpeulen, 2026-08-13: the ladder needs to span **500 um down to angstrom**
without losing accuracy at float32. That is a wider ask than the objective's
stated "roughly 1 angstrom to 10 micrometres, five orders of magnitude" —
500 um / 1 A = 5x10^6, **6.7 decades (22.25 octaves)**, about 1.7 decades (50x)
past the 10 um figure the constraints section sized "float32 carries 10 um
with about two orders of headroom" against. That headroom claim was for 10 um
and should not be assumed to still hold at 500 um without re-deriving it.

The raw number, so it is checkable rather than asserted: float32's mantissa
gives a relative precision of 2^-24, about 6x10^-8. A single flat frame with
positions in metres, at the 500 um edge of the scene, resolves to
`5x10^-4 m x 6x10^-8 ~= 3x10^-11 m`, i.e. **about 0.3 A** — under 1 A in
isolation, but that is the optimistic case: it is the ULP at rest, before any
matrix-transform chain, before subtracting two large nearly-equal positions
(the classic large-world jitter bug), and before the GPU vertex/depth pipeline
does its own arithmetic on the same numbers. **One flat float32 world frame is
not a safe design for this span**, whatever margin it has in isolation.

The proposal is not new mechanism — it is generalising two things this PRD
already does **locally** into the **global** addressing scheme:

- **Potree's fixed-point-relative-to-node-bbox** (constraints point 3): every
  position stored relative to its own node's local origin, in a fixed
  bit-count, so no coordinate anywhere in the structure ever has to represent
  the full 500 um span at angstrom precision — only its own node's local
  extent has to.
- **Part 4.3's page-relative `u16[3]` quantisation**: the same move, already
  designed for the splat rung's own page tree.

Generalised, that is a literal pyramid: a top level tiling the ~500 um scene
bound, each level storing positions only relative to its own local origin,
cascading down to a leaf (a rung-3 superatom cluster, a rung-1 residue) small
enough that fixed-point at that leaf's scale is angstrom-exact rather than
approximate. Camera-relative rebasing at render time — already named in the
objective's list of traps — is the same idea applied once more, at the very
top: the GPU should never be handed a raw 500 um-scale coordinate at all.

Constraints point 4's VDB configuration (`[7,6,5,4,3]`, 7.5 orders of
magnitude in 439 MB) already has more than enough headroom for 6.7 decades —
but a VDB indexes *space*, and the constraints section already says outright
that "a VDB is explicitly not a mip pyramid." Turning that spatial index into
the actual quantised-position pyramid is the design gap, not the index itself.

**tpeulen, 2026-08-13, extending the idea: maybe fp16, scale-dependent** —
i.e. a leaf encoding whose exponent/scale tracks the pyramid level, rather
than a fixed-point integer. Worth stating precisely rather than by feel, since
the two are not free substitutes for one another at equal bit width. fp16 is
1 sign + 5 exponent + 10 mantissa bits, an effective 11-bit mantissa once the
implicit leading one is counted, so its *relative* precision is a constant
`2^-11 ~= 4.9x10^-4` (~0.05%) at any magnitude within its exponent range — that
is the "scale-dependent" part: one format covers many scales for free, which a
fixed-point integer cannot (it is exact but only over the one box it was
quantised against). The cost is worst-case *absolute* precision at equal bit
width: a 16-bit fixed-point coordinate spends all 16 bits as mantissa, so for
a local node of extent `L` its worst-case error is `L / 2^16`, against fp16's
`L x 2^-11` — **fixed-point is ~32x tighter than fp16 for the same node size**,
because float gives 5 of its 16 bits to an exponent the pyramid's own levels
already provide. Concretely: to guarantee sub-angstrom absolute error, an
fp16-encoded leaf needs its local node extent under roughly
`1 A / 4.9x10^-4 ~= 2049 A ~= 0.2 um`, where a 16-bit-fixed-point leaf (as
Potree and part 4.3 already do it) only needs to get under roughly
`1 A x 2^16 ~= 6.5 um` — about 32x coarser, i.e. far fewer pyramid levels for
the same guarantee. Note also that "scale-dependent fp16" and "fixed-point
relative to a per-node scale" (the scheme already proposed above) are close
cousins — both are a shared per-node exponent plus a mantissa, which is
exactly what block-floating-point / shared-exponent formats are, and part
4.3's own scale field (log-scale `u8[3]`) is already that idea in miniature
for a Gaussian's *size*, not yet for its *position*. The real discriminator is
practical, not mathematical: whether native GPU `f16` (WGSL's `shader-f16`
feature) decodes cheaper in a shader than unpacking a fixed-point integer by
hand — **unverified on this adapter**, unlike `shader-int64` and the other
features this PRD already checked and recorded.

What is unresolved:

- **One pyramid, or one per rung?** Part 4 already builds a page tree for the
  splat rung alone. Whether this coordinate pyramid is that same structure
  generalised across all four rungs, or a separate spatial index every rung's
  geometry is expressed relative to, changes what gets built once versus four
  times.
- **Fixed levels or adaptive?** VDB's `[7,6,5,4,3]` is a fixed configuration;
  Potree's adaptive version quantises to each batch's own bbox. Molecular
  structure is not uniformly dense across 6.7 decades (an atom is not a
  space-filling voxel grid), so the right answer is unlikely to be either
  extreme unmodified.
- **Fixed-point or scale-dependent fp16 at the leaf?** The numbers above say
  fixed-point wants ~32x fewer, larger nodes for the same accuracy; fp16's
  case rests entirely on whether `shader-f16` is present and cheap on the
  target adapters, which has not been checked.
- **Where does rebasing actually happen?** In the CPU-side data structure (so
  nothing above local-node precision is ever stored), only at render time
  (camera-relative, structure stored however), or both — these are different
  amounts of work and fail differently.
- **No measurement yet.** Same caveat as part 5: this is a parking spot for
  the idea, not a design, and every number above is back-of-envelope, not
  measured on this stack.

## 7. Draft idea: generative gap-filling from sparse multi-scale experimental data (2026-08-13, not scoped)

tpeulen, 2026-08-13: **large parts of a gigastructure will start unfilled.**
bentopy's `placements.json` (part 3) only places *known* components — it says
where a copy of something already built goes, not what fills the space where
nothing is resolved. For most of a cell-scale scene, most of the volume is
exactly that: no crystal structure, no cryo-EM map, nothing but the fact that
*something* physically occupies it. That gap needs a **generative process**,
not just an assembler, and what it generates has to be informed by whatever
**sparse experimental data exists at that region's own scale** — because the
data available differs by scale in exactly the way the ladder already does:
atomic-resolution evidence (crystallography, high-resolution cryo-EM,
sequence/homology) at rung 1; distance-restraint evidence (crosslinking-MS,
FRET, EPR, SAXS/SANS shape envelopes) spanning rung 1-2; and low-resolution
shape evidence (cryo-ET, soft X-ray tomography, confocal/light microscopy
envelopes) at rung 3-4. This is not a new taxonomy — it is IMP's own classic
integrative-modelling data inventory, restated against this PRD's ladder
rather than invented fresh, and IMP.pmi already carries the working principle
that *representation should match the resolution of the available data*.

**The specific ask: drive that choice by something like Minimum Description
Length**, rather than a fixed per-rung rule or hand-tuned heuristic. The shape
of it — pick the representation, and how much is generated versus placed
versus atomistically known, that best trades model complexity against fit to
whatever sparse data actually constrains that region — formalises the
resolution-matches-data principle IMP.pmi already states informally into an
actual selection objective. Nothing in this stack has tried it; it is recorded
here as the target criterion, not a chosen method.

Where it sits: this is assembly-side, the same seam part 3 already draws —
**imp-tricks builds, ChiMOL displays and drives.** A generative fill is
something imp-tricks would produce (from statistical/physical priors —
excluded volume, self-avoiding-walk statistics for unmodelled loops, membrane
or compartment geometry, crowding — at minimum, with any learned generative
approach an open and unproven question at this scale, not assumed). ChiMOL's
side is showing the result honestly: which regions are observed, which are
placed-but-unrefined, and which are generatively filled, so a filled gap is
never mistaken for a measurement. That is a new field on the interchange
format part 3 already flags as needing decided early — provenance/confidence
per element, not only geometry and instance hierarchy.

### 7.1 Model the generating process, not the structure (2026-08-13 refinement)

tpeulen, 2026-08-13, sharpening the objective above: **a probabilistic
integrative-modelling process should infer the generating process, not the
structure.** The target is not "which structure(s) fit the sparse
constraints" — it is "which *process* would generate structures consistent
with the constraints," with any actual structure recovered afterward as one
sample drawn from that inferred process, not the object of inference itself.

This has a name and an existing formalism, and it is worth reading rather than
reinventing: **Maximum Caliber** (Jaynes 1980; surveyed in Pressé, Ghosh, Lee
& Dill, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* 85, 1115, 2013 — cited by name and year only, not
yet read against this PRD, so treat the citation as a pointer to chase rather
than a verified primary source). MaxCal is the dynamical analogue of Maximum
Entropy: instead of finding the least-biased *distribution over states*
consistent with given expectation constraints (MaxEnt), it finds the
least-biased *distribution over paths* (trajectories, i.e. generative
histories) consistent with given path-ensemble constraints — which is exactly
"determine the generating process, given the constraints/restraints." **Yes to
path integrals**: MaxCal's solution takes a path-integral form, a probability
over an entire trajectory weighted by `exp(-action)` (the discrete-time
version is a product of local transition probabilities), with Lagrange
multipliers conjugate to each imposed constraint reweighting the path
ensemble — mathematically the same shape as the Onsager-Machlup action for a
stochastic (Langevin/diffusive) process.

Two things make this less alien to this PRD than it first sounds:

- **A path integral is already the proposed baseline, just not named as one.**
  The Wiener/Edwards path integral for Gaussian-chain statistics (generalised
  to self-avoiding chains) is the standard formulation behind exactly the
  "self-avoiding-walk statistics for unmodelled loops" prior part 7 already
  names above. MaxCal generalises the same object: instead of a bare polymer
  prior, it is a path ensemble additionally reweighted by whatever sparse
  experimental restraints apply, and instead of one loop it would in principle
  cover the generative process across the whole ladder.
- **It may be the operational answer to "what does MDL across scales mean"**
  (an open question below): a MaxCal/MaxEnt path ensemble's entropy term is
  already a complexity penalty in the same spirit as description length — the
  solution is the *simplest* (highest-entropy, least-committed) process
  consistent with the data. Whether that correspondence is exact enough to
  serve as the MDL currency this PRD already asked for, or only a family
  resemblance, has not been checked.

What this reframing costs, stated plainly rather than left implicit: inferring
a distribution over *processes* is a strictly harder problem than inferring a
distribution over *structures*, which is already what IMP.pmi's Bayesian
structural sampling does today. Path-ensemble / transition-path-sampling-style
inference is known to be expensive even for a single well-defined dynamical
system; doing it across a multi-scale ladder, for an entire gigastructure, is
unproven at any scale relevant here. This section records the objective and
the formalism it matches, not a feasibility claim.

### 7.2 Grounding 7.1: dynamics, folded in from existing practice (2026-08-13)

tpeulen asked to "fold in dynamics," pointing at Gusew, Hansen & Lindorff-Larsen,
*"Integrative modelling of biomolecular dynamics,"* arXiv:2510.01108 (Univ. of
Copenhagen) — **read**, not merely named, unlike 7.1's own MaxCal citation.
It is a review of exactly the family 7.1 gestured at, with real named methods
and results rather than one formalism in isolation, and it fixes something
7.1 was missing: a piece connecting sparse experimental data to a candidate
generative process.

- **Forward models** — "calculate an experimental observable from either a
  structure or a trajectory" — are the missing piece part 7 needed and never
  named: one forward model per data modality (SAXS, NMR relaxation, cryo-EM,
  HDX-MS, time-resolved crystallography) is what actually lets a candidate
  process, or an MDL/MaxCal score, be checked against real sparse restraints
  rather than an abstract "fit to data."
- **BME (Bayesian/Maximum-Entropy reweighting)** — reweights an existing
  structural ensemble to better match experimental averages, trading fit
  against divergence from the prior ensemble. A concrete, already-published,
  *ensemble*-level instance of the fit-vs-complexity trade 7.1 hoped MaxCal's
  entropy term would supply — one level down from full path-ensemble
  inference, and worth naming as the cheaper fallback if 7.1's full version
  turns out intractable.
- **Markov State Models (MSMs)** — discretise configuration space, estimate
  transition probabilities from many short parallel MD runs, and reconstruct
  slow dynamics beyond any single trajectory's length. An MSM *is* a coarse
  generative process inferred from data — a second, likely cheaper, concrete
  candidate for "what actually generates the fill" (part 7's open question),
  distinct from full MaxCal path reweighting.
- **CoPE-MaxCal** — reweights transition-path ensembles to match experimental
  rate constants while minimally perturbing the path distribution, and
  reportedly predicted more native-like transition-state ensembles for
  peptide folding. This is 7.1's Maximum Caliber idea, already implemented
  and published with a real result — the concrete instance to read next if
  7.1 is pursued, rather than the Jaynes/Pressé formalism cold.
- **dynAMMo** — integrates NMR observables directly into MSM estimation,
  improving kinetic predictions even for rare or unsampled states. This is
  the closest published precedent specifically for gap-filling: improving
  predictions where data is sparse or absent, using an inferred generative
  process, at protein-folding scale. If any one paper here should be read for
  architecture before building part 7, it is this one.
- **trBME** — Langevin dynamics on a low-dimensional free-energy surface
  (from enhanced sampling), with time rescaled to match a time-resolved
  experimental signal (demonstrated on synthetic temperature-jump SAXS
  unfolding data). A small, real, worked prototype of exactly "process, not
  structure," coupled to time-resolved sparse data.

**The honest limit, stated as plainly as 7.1's own cost paragraph:** every
system here is single-protein or single-domain scale — ubiquitin, BPTI,
chignolin, T4 lysozyme, bovine serum albumin — atomistic MD, femtosecond-to-
millisecond timescales. Nothing in this review touches a multi-scale ladder,
cell-scale assembly, splatting, paging, or attention-coupling. It grounds the
*formalism* 7.1 proposed in real, working, cited methods; it is not evidence
that any of them scale to a gigastructure. Treat it as the reading list for
part 7's "what actually generates the fill" question, not as a solved
problem.

**tpeulen, 2026-08-13: "a bit like the Elite game."** The reference is
*Elite* (1984, Braben/Bell): its galaxy of thousands of star systems was never
stored — each system's properties (name, economy, government, position) were
a deterministic function of a small per-system seed, run through a
pseudo-random generator, so an entire galaxy fit in a machine with kilobytes
of memory and a system's contents came into existence, identically, only when
a player actually reached it. The part worth borrowing is not procedural
content for its own sake, it is the **shape of the mechanism**: generated
content as a *pure, deterministic function of (region identity, seed, and the
sparse constraints known for that region)*, computed on arrival rather than
stored — which is precisely how part 4's page-request queue and part 5's
attention idea already want to work, extended one step further. Instead of a
page fetch pulling stored geometry off disk, an unfilled page's request
*generates* its content on demand from the seed and whatever sparse
experimental data constrains it; leaving attention regenerates it later
identically rather than needing to keep it resident or ever have stored it in
full. This is what would let a gigastructure's unfilled majority cost roughly
nothing at rest, the same way Elite's galaxy did — but note the disanalogy
too: Elite's per-system generator had no external data to be consistent
with, while gap-filling here must reproduce the *same* MDL-selected fill
against the *same* sparse constraints every time a region is revisited, which
is a harder determinism requirement than a space-trading game ever had to
meet.

What is unresolved:

- **What actually generates the fill?** Physical/statistical priors are the
  known-safe baseline (IMP already has machinery in this direction). Whether
  a learned generative model applies at cell/organelle scale the way it does
  for single proteins is a different, unproven question and should not be
  assumed by analogy. 7.1 proposes Maximum Caliber / path-ensemble inference
  as the formalism; 7.2 supplies concrete, published, protein-scale instances
  to read before choosing one — MSMs and dynAMMo as the likely-cheaper
  candidates, CoPE-MaxCal as 7.1's own idea already implemented — none
  verified past single-domain scale.
- **What does "MDL across scales" mean operationally?** A description-length
  criterion needs a shared currency to trade complexity against fit across
  representations that are qualitatively different (atoms vs. beads vs.
  splats) — 7.1 notes MaxCal's path-entropy term may already play this role,
  which is a family resemblance to check, not an established equivalence.
- **Does inferring a process rather than a structure actually pay for its own
  cost?** 7.1 names the escalation plainly: this is a strictly harder
  inference problem than IMP.pmi's existing structure-level Bayesian
  sampling, and path-ensemble inference is expensive even for one
  well-defined dynamical system. Whether the multi-scale, whole-gigastructure
  version is tractable at all is unknown.
- **Does this couple to attention (part 5)?** Generatively filling a gap in
  full detail everywhere is presumably as wasteful as simulating dynamics
  everywhere; whether gap-filling should itself be driven finer only where
  currently observed is a natural but unstated extension of part 5's idea.
- **The interchange format grows a field.** Part 3 already requires the
  imp-tricks/ChiMOL interchange to carry the instance hierarchy; this adds
  that it must also carry provenance — observed vs. placed vs. generated —
  per element.
- **Generate-on-demand or generate-once-and-cache?** The Elite analogy only
  pays off if regenerating a page is cheap enough to redo on every re-entry
  into attention; if the generative process (or the MDL search over it) turns
  out expensive, the right move may be closer to Elite's own actual practice
  of caching whatever the player has already visited than to regenerating
  blindly. Which it is has not been measured, because nothing has been built.
- **No measurement yet.** Same caveat as parts 5 and 6: a parking spot for the
  idea, not a design.

**The baseline exists now** (2026-08-12). On an Apple M1 Pro, offscreen
WebGPU/Metal, the `npc_integrative` demo at 234,184 beads:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| total load | 113.2 s | **9.3 s** |
| `fetch PDBDEV_00000012` | 71.7 s | 7.8 s |
| `spectrum molecule` | 41.4 s | 1.5 s |

Re-derive it with the profiling harness — build `ChimolApp(backend="offscreen")`
under `CHIMOL_TOOLKIT=none`, run the three demo commands under `cProfile`, and
**sort by `tottime`, not `cumtime`**: all three costs found were self-time in one
Python loop, and a cumulative sort buries them under their callers. The three
fixes and the reasoning are in
[known-issues](../references/known-issues.md).

Two traps in taking this measurement, both of which cost time:

* The **cached fetch matters.** With `~/.chisurf/structures/chimol/` primed the
  number is load-and-build; cold it includes a download and is not comparable.
* **Frame time is still unmeasured.** `CanvasView` has no `draw()`, so the
  obvious harness silently measures nothing. Peak VRAM is unmeasured too. Both
  belong in the benchmark row this PRD asks for, and neither is captured by the
  load-time number above — a structure can load fast and draw at 2 fps.

Next, in order — **reordered 2026-08-12 by measurement**, which put the CPU
first and demoted instancing:

1. **Per-repaint CPU work.** The frame is 94 % chrome; the molecule is ~1-2 ms.
   Nothing else on this list matters until a frame stops being rebuilt from
   scratch in Python. A dirty-flag cache on the chrome quad buffer is the whole
   job — the chrome changes on hover, focus and state, not on camera motion, and
   camera motion is when frames matter.
2. **LOD**, because it is what buys frame rate: Mol*'s own ablation is 10-26x,
   and its stride-ordered buffer makes selecting a level a `drawCount`
   truncation rather than a re-upload.
3. **Instancing**, for memory, build time and residency — not for frame rate.
   It is still needed (it is what makes a gigastructure *fit*), and it is the
   prerequisite for a scene that carries a hierarchy rather than baked
   coordinates.

Two things worth doing **before** any of that, because both are cheap, neither
depends on the rest, and both get harder to retrofit:

* **Reversed-Z + `depth32float` + infinite far plane.** One change, well
  evidenced at exactly this near/far ratio, and the ladder is unusable without
  it — see the constraints section. Note float depth alone buys nothing; it must
  be paired with reversed-Z.
* **Settle the 64-bit atomic question** against whatever rung-3/4 design is
  chosen. It is verified absent on this M1, so a Nanite- or Schutz-shaped
  visibility buffer needs a different packing before it is designed, not after.
2. **Consume bentopy's `placements.json`** as the instance list. That is the same
   data structure instancing needs, so the assembler and the hierarchy arrive
   together — and it means not writing a packer.
3. **Pet representation as meshes/instances, not as Martini.** The assessment
   above is why: Martini is ~5x too weak and has no coarser tier.

What is left of the earlier RMF round — interior culling and dynamic LOD — is
still open and still the right next thing after instancing.

The failure mode to expect throughout: **a per-item Python loop that is
unremarkable at a few thousand atoms and is the entire runtime at a few hundred
thousand.** All three NPC costs were that, none was an algorithm anyone chose,
and 30x the NPC will surface more of them.
