# Gigastructures in ChiMOL

## The objective, stated

tpeulen, 2026-08-12: **YASARA's performance is the baseline ChiMOL has to
match.** A gigastructure is the test case, and ChiMOL must handle systems up to
**30x the size of the NPC** — the NPC demo being the thing that is still slow
today. Two further asks came with it: **support pet molecules**, and be able to
**assemble gigastructures in ChiMOL using IMP**.

## What the baseline actually is

From YASARA's own pages, so the target is a number rather than an impression:

* A model of the **presynaptic bouton with 3.6 billion atoms**, visualised
  **interactively** on a single **GeForce RTX 2080**.
* More than **4 GB of graphics memory** for the largest structures.
* The reduction comes from two independent compressions, and the factors are
  quoted separately: **coarse-graining ~50x**, **GPU instancing 40-1000x**.

That last line is the whole design. Neither trick alone reaches 3.6 billion:
50x off 3.6e9 is still 72 million, which no immediate-mode renderer draws
interactively. It is the *product* — coarse-graining the geometry and then
drawing each distinct piece once per frame however many copies exist — that
closes the gap. Any plan that adopts one and defers the other will miss the
target by roughly the factor it deferred.

## What a pet molecule is

YASARA's **"pet world"**: molecules scaled to **1/10th** their normal size and
represented by **pet atoms** — coarse-grained beads that need only **~2% of the
original atom count**. Beads are coloured by size, 0.2 to 1.2 Angstrom, so the
simplification is legible rather than hidden.

Two properties matter for scoping, and both are easy to get wrong:

1. **It is a modelling representation, not an approximation of dynamics.**
   YASARA is explicit that pet-world simulation is *not* a faster replacement
   for all-atom simulation — it is **aimed at model building only**. A pet
   structure is how you *assemble* and *inspect* something enormous; it is not
   a cheaper way to compute its physics. ChiMOL should carry the same warning
   at the point of use, or someone will publish a pet-world energy.
2. **The scale factor is part of the representation.** Coordinates live at 1/10
   size. ChiMOL already has a scene scale factor of 10 and a documented trap
   around it (a radius of gyration read off scene coordinates came out 10x
   too large). Pet coordinates entering that pipeline are a second factor of
   ten waiting to multiply with the first.

## The three parts

### 1. Instancing over the copy hierarchy

The NPC is many copies of a few distinct components; a gigastructure is that
taken much further. The change is to stop treating a copy as unique geometry:
**one built mesh per distinct type, drawn N times with per-instance
transforms**, rather than N built meshes. That collapses build time *and* VRAM
by the copy count, which is the only kind of factor that reaches 30x.

Prerequisite: the scene must carry a *hierarchy* with instance transforms
rather than a flat list of objects. Read **ChimeraX** (`junk/ChimeraX`) for how
a molecular viewer does this at scale before designing — it is already this
plugin's reference for density-map contouring, and its `CHISURF-*` headers
record what has been taken from it.

### 2. The pet representation

A bead per group of atoms at 1/10 scale, drawn with the existing analytic
sphere impostor (spheres are already 1,299 quads and 0 triangles for 148L, so
the primitive is right). What is new is the *reduction*: which atoms map to
which bead, and the radius/colour rule.

### 3. Assembly, in imp-tricks — not here

Building a gigastructure rather than only importing one. **tpeulen, 2026-08-12:
this is to be implemented in imp-tricks**, and that is a scope boundary rather
than a preference. It follows the rule the project already applies to every
other piece of molecular modelling — AV, labels, restraints, Structure,
ProteinMC, kinetics all left chisurf for `IMP.cgmol.*` / `IMP.bff.*` — and it
draws the seam in the useful place:

* **imp-tricks builds.** Coarse-grained representation, packing, collision
  detection, restraints, sampling. It already owns IMP.
* **ChiMOL displays and drives.** It loads what imp-tricks writes and gives the
  operation a viewport, a command and a progress report. It does not learn to
  pack.

The practical consequence is that the *interchange format* is the real interface
between the two, and it needs deciding early: whatever imp-tricks emits has to
carry the instance hierarchy (part 1) rather than a flattened particle list, or
the assembler will destroy exactly the structure the renderer needs.

**Leverage Martini** (tpeulen, 2026-08-12), and imp-tricks already has Martini
support to start from. The assessment was done — findings below, and two of them
change the plan.

#### Martini is not a pet-molecule substitute

This is the load-bearing result, and it contradicts the obvious reading of the
brief. **There is no coarser-than-4:1 Martini.** Neither Dry Martini (implicit
solvent) nor GōMartini 3 changes the mapping, and the S and T bead classes go the
*finer* direction — 3:1 and 2:1 — not coarser. Martini 3's effective compression
measured against real published systems is **9–11 heavy atoms per bead**
(triply sourced: a whole cell at 10.7, SARS-CoV-2 at 8.9, and 561 M beads against
>6 G atoms at ~11, water dominating because one `W` bead is four waters).

YASARA's pet world claims ~2 % of the atom count, i.e. roughly **50:1**. Martini
is therefore about **5x weaker compression**, and no configuration of it closes
that gap. A plan that adopts Martini *as* the pet representation misses the
target by that factor.

The Marrink lab's own route above Martini resolution is to **leave particle-based
modelling entirely**: a dynamically triangulated continuum surface
([FreeDTS](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-44819-w)), backmapped down into
Martini beads by [TS2CG](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-16094-y). The
ecosystem's answer above Martini is a **mesh**, not a smaller bead.

So the design is **two levels**, which is what the instancing section was already
reaching toward:

* **display / assembly level** — meshes and instance transforms, ChiMOL's pet
  analogue, where the 50x lives;
* **Martini level** — beads with real parameters, for anything physical.

If a genuinely coarser *force field* is wanted rather than a display
representation, the documented precedent is
[SMARTINI3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-75490-2) (~3 beads per lipid
against Martini's ~12 for POPC), explicitly built so Martini proteins embed in an
ultra-CG bilayer. Note it is Risselada's group, not Marrink's.

#### The assembly tool already exists: bentopy

This is what "there was already a tool" refers to. **bentopy** — Westendorp,
Stevens, Brown, Dommer, Wassenaar, Bruininks & Marrink, *Protein Science* 35(3):e70480
(2026), [10.1002/pro.70480](https://doi.org/10.1002/pro.70480) — packs large
cellular scenes and emits a **`placements.json`**: a list of *which component goes
where*. That is an instance list, which is precisely the input part 1 needs, so
adopting it and consuming `placements.json` gets the assembler and the instance
hierarchy in one move. Do not write a packer.

Division of labour: **bentopy places**, **imp-tricks builds the components and
refines with IMP restraints**, **ChiMOL displays the instances**.

#### The physics claim, stated precisely

A Martini-shaped model is a candidate for simulation **only via an exported
GROMACS topology**, and *not* via imp-tricks' in-tree scoring as currently
written. The assessment found the in-tree interaction table is a five-value
lookup on a bead's first letter, where real Martini 3 has thousands of distinct
epsilon values — every one of P1…P6 collapses to one number. That is not an
approximation of Martini 3, it is a different force field. State the seam; do not
let a picture imply an energy.

#### Licensing

Martini 3 parameters are **Apache 2.0** via
[marrink-lab/martini-forcefields](https://github.com/marrink-lab/martini-forcefields).
**cgmartini.nl carries no license at all** — vendor and cite from the GitHub
grant, never the website. Pin the *molecule* parameter set, not "Martini 3": the
bead interaction matrix has not moved since v3.0.0 in 2021 and there are no
tagged releases, while the lipid and IDP parameter sets move independently.

YASARA's own assembly path is the shape to aim at: nucleic acids and whole
genomes built **directly from a FASTA file**, optionally with secondary structure
in **dot-bracket** notation, with efficient collision detection producing compact
packings.

## Definition of done

- [ ] A gigastructure test case exists in the repo's benchmark set, with its
      atom count recorded, and `docs/development/benchmarks.md` carries a row
      for load time, frame time and peak VRAM.
- [ ] The NPC demo's load and frame times are measured **before** any change,
      so the 30x claim can be checked rather than asserted.
- [ ] Instanced drawing: a scene with N copies of one type builds one mesh and
      draws N, with a test asserting the built-geometry count does not scale
      with N.
- [ ] Pet representation: an all-atom structure reduces to pet beads at ~2% of
      the atom count, at 1/10 scale, with the scale factor applied exactly once
      (a regression test on radius of gyration, which is what caught the last
      double-scaling).
- [ ] Pet-world modelling carries YASARA's own caveat where a user meets it:
      model building only, not a faster simulation.
- [x] An assessment of imp-tricks' existing Martini support against what a
      gigastructure needs, written down before any new assembly code — including
      whether the existing external gigastructure assembler can be used rather
      than reimplemented. **Done 2026-08-12**; the answers are above and two of
      them changed the plan (Martini is not the pet layer; bentopy is the packer).
- [ ] The in-tree Martini interaction table is either corrected against the
      shipped Apache-2.0 parameters or explicitly demoted to a display-only
      representation that never reports an energy. It cannot stay as it is and
      be called Martini.
- [ ] Assembly from a FASTA file with dot-bracket secondary structure produces
      a packed structure through IMP. **Implemented in imp-tricks**, committed
      there; ChiMOL only loads and drives it.
- [ ] The interchange format between imp-tricks and ChiMOL carries the instance
      hierarchy, not a flattened particle list.
- [ ] A `docs/concepts/` page on what the pet representation means and a
      numbered `docs/guides/` workflow for building one, per the project's
      plugin-documentation rule.

## Where to pick this up

**The baseline exists now** (2026-08-12). On an Apple M1 Pro, offscreen
WebGPU/Metal, the `npc_integrative` demo at 234,184 beads:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| total load | 113.2 s | **9.3 s** |
| `fetch PDBDEV_00000012` | 71.7 s | 7.8 s |
| `spectrum molecule` | 41.4 s | 1.5 s |

Re-derive it with the profiling harness — build `ChimolApp(backend="offscreen")`
under `CHIMOL_TOOLKIT=none`, run the three demo commands under `cProfile`, and
**sort by `tottime`, not `cumtime`**: all three costs found were self-time in one
Python loop, and a cumulative sort buries them under their callers. The three
fixes and the reasoning are in
[known-issues](../references/known-issues.md).

Two traps in taking this measurement, both of which cost time:

* The **cached fetch matters.** With `~/.chisurf/structures/chimol/` primed the
  number is load-and-build; cold it includes a download and is not comparable.
* **Frame time is still unmeasured.** `CanvasView` has no `draw()`, so the
  obvious harness silently measures nothing. Peak VRAM is unmeasured too. Both
  belong in the benchmark row this PRD asks for, and neither is captured by the
  load-time number above — a structure can load fast and draw at 2 fps.

Next, in order:

1. **Instancing**, before pet and before Martini. Larger factor, independent of
   the representation, and it is what makes a mesoscale structure *drawable*
   rather than merely smaller. Read ChimeraX for how a molecular viewer does it
   at scale.
2. **Consume bentopy's `placements.json`** as the instance list. That is the same
   data structure instancing needs, so the assembler and the hierarchy arrive
   together — and it means not writing a packer.
3. **Pet representation as meshes/instances, not as Martini.** The assessment
   above is why: Martini is ~5x too weak and has no coarser tier.

What is left of the earlier RMF round — interior culling and dynamic LOD — is
still open and still the right next thing after instancing.

The failure mode to expect throughout: **a per-item Python loop that is
unremarkable at a few thousand atoms and is the entire runtime at a few hundred
thousand.** All three NPC costs were that, none was an algorithm anyone chose,
and 30x the NPC will surface more of them.
